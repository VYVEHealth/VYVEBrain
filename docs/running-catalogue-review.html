What the engine enforces on top of these templates. Three ceilings are applied at build time, per week, from Daniels' guidance: no more than 10% of the week's distance at threshold, 8% at interval (and never more than 10km), 5% at repetition, and no long run above 30% of the week. They are enforced by trimming rep counts, never rep distances — an 800 is always an 800. Below about 25km a week the percentages would leave too little to be a training stimulus at all, so each band also has a floor and ceiling in minutes: threshold lands between 20 and 30 minutes, interval between 12 and 15. So the same template yields fewer reps for a low-mileage member than the tables below show at full volume.
How the paces work. Sessions never store a pace figure — they reference one of five bands, and the band is resolved per member from their race time using Daniels' VDOT method. A member who runs 25:00 for 5k (VDOT 38.3) sees easy 6:11–6:54/km, threshold 5:17–5:28/km, interval 4:45–5:01/km, rep 4:29–4:40/km — against Daniels' published figures for that VDOT of easy 6:10–6:53, threshold 5:19, interval 4:54, rep about 4:35. Long runs are run at easy pace; "long" is a label, not a separate intensity. A faster member sees faster numbers from exactly the same session. Limit means "no faster than this"; target means "run at this".

What scales and what doesn't. Repeated efforts hold their named distance at every volume setting — 800s are always 800m, a stride is always 100m, a broken mile is always a mile. Warm-ups, cool-downs, easy runs and long runs absorb the week multiplier and the member's own volume setting.
