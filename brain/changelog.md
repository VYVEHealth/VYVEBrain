**PM-1244 (2026-09-13): RUNNING WAVE 2, PART 1 — A PLAN LENGTH IS NOW A REAL QUESTION. `run-engine` v9 (`run-engine@0.5.2`), migration `pm1244_run_phase_expansion`. Server only, nothing member-facing.**

PM-1243 moved variable plan length forward into Wave 2 because the wizard asks "how many weeks" and every template was locked to exactly one. `buildPlan` clamped to a template's min/max and needed an authored row for every week up to the max, so a 26-week plan meant 130 hand-authored week rows.

**Phase-based expansion.** A template is now authored as an optional **head** (intro weeks), a repeating **cycle**, and a fixed **tail** (peak + taper), carried on five columns (`expansion_mode`, `cycle_start_week`, `cycle_end_week`, `cycle_growth_step`, `cycle_growth_cap`). A new pure function `weekScript(template, weeks)` returns which authored week each built week draws from; head and tail are always kept in authored order and the cycle repeats or truncates to reach the requested length. The built plan carries `week_script` so the mapping is visible in the response rather than inferred. Ranges now live: **`5k-improver` 6–20** (head w1, cycle w2–5, tail w6–8), **`10k-build` 8–24** (head w1–3, cycle w4–7, tail w8–10), **`general-training` 4–26** (the whole 12-week arc is the cycle — a short plan is its opening weeks, a long one repeats the arc). **`start-running` stays FIXED at 9** on purpose: it is the NHS Couch to 5K structure verbatim (§23.349), and stretching a reference plan we deliberately copied is re-authoring it.

**Two defects the proof run found, one of them pre-dating this wave.**

**(1) The peak stopped being the peak.** Each cycle repetition adds `growth_step` to its volume (5k/10k 5% per repeat capped at 1.20, general training 4% capped at 1.12), but the first cut left the tail at authored volume — so a 24-week 10k reached 37.3km in its last cycle week and then "peaked" at 33.3km, reading as a three-week taper with a peak week buried in it. **The tail now inherits the final repetition's growth**: the same build peaks at week 23 (38.9km) and tapers to 19.3km.

**(2) A three-day week's long run was its shortest run — and this was live before today.** `10k-build` is authored for five days; a member on three keeps slots 1–3, and Daniels' 30% long-run ceiling then capped the long run at 4.9km inside a 16.4km week while an uncapped easy run came out at 5.6km. Proven pre-existing by building the authored 10-week length at three days on the same engine: identical fault, every week. **The ceiling is now days-aware** — 40% at three days, 35% at four, 30% at five or more (§23.351). Every week of every build in the final sweep has the long run as its longest session, including the low-volume injury-adjusted case that first showed it.

**Live proof (`pg_net`, engine v9).** Thirteen invocations: health reports `run-engine@0.5.2`; every plan built at its minimum and maximum days per week across short, authored and stretched lengths (5k 6/8/20, 10k 8/16/24, general 4/26, start-running 9); determinism re-proven post-deploy (two identical `5k-improver` 20-week builds, md5 `bb1d4e5959adab27fd154448a0d704d6`); no-key and wrong-key both 401; a 40-week request clamps to 20 and a 20-week request on `start-running` returns 9. **PM-1241 (2026-09-13): THE RUNNING CATALOGUE WAS WRONG AND IS NOW RIGHT — `run-engine` v6, PACE BANDS CORRECTED, DANIELS CEILINGS ENFORCED, START RUNNING REBUILT ON NHS C25K.** *(PM number collides with the parallel coach-portal session's PM-1241 session-tabs entry; migrations here are `pm1241_start_running_nhs_shape` and the EF is `run-engine@0.4.0`.)*

**Dean's challenge, and it was right: why is this aimed at Calum? He is a PT, not a running coach.** The gate came from PM-1237's spec line ("Calum — plan templates, session library, progression rules") and PM-1240 carried it forward without asking whether endurance periodisation is his domain. It is not. Runna's Ben Parker is a PT **and** an England Athletics Coach **and** an IRONMAN coach, and they still put a GB Olympian on the coaching team — being a PT is the least relevant of those qualifications for this work. **The right reviewer is an England Athletics Coach in Running Fitness (CiRF)** — the off-track endurance award, assessed on technical knowledge, physical preparation, planning and a submitted session plan + mesocycle, and the level at which a coach is insured to work unsupervised. Calum keeps the strength-alongside work, where he is qualified and where the evidence is strongest.

**Then a full literature audit of PM-1240's own catalogue, which found three defects and one strategic miss.**

**1. The pace bands were wrong — a units error in Wave 0.** Daniels publishes his zones as fractions of **VO2 uptake** (E 59–74%, T ~88%, I 95–100%); Wave 0 used those as fractions of **velocity**, which is only near-correct at the top of the range. For a 25:00 5k (VDOT 38.3, vVDOT 4:45/km) we were prescribing easy 6:36–7:40/km against Daniels' 6:10–6:53, and threshold 5:24–5:44 against 5:19 — every easy run 25–45 s/km too slow and every duration estimate ~10% inflated. Interval and rep were fine by luck. **v5 fractions solved backwards from the published table: easy 0.77–0.69, threshold 0.90–0.87, interval 1.00–0.95, rep 1.06–1.02**, which now return easy 6:11–6:54, threshold 5:17–5:28, interval 4:45–5:01, rep 4:29–4:40. **The separate `long` band was an invention and is retired** — Daniels runs long runs at E pace, so `long` carries the easy range and survives only as a display label.

**2. Quality volume was roughly double Daniels' per-session ceilings.** His limits are T ≤10% of weekly distance, I ≤8% (and ≤10km), R ≤5%, long run ≤25–30%. Rolling 800s was putting 4.8km of interval into a 30km week (16%); Threshold Blocks and Broken Miles 4.8km of threshold into a 26km week (18%). **The engine now enforces all four, by trimming REP COUNTS and never rep distances** — PM-1240 got half of this right (an 800 stays an 800) and drew the wrong conclusion from it (that quality shouldn't scale at all). It is the *count* that scales: a 20km week gets three 800s, a 40km week gets six.

**3. And the percentage alone is wrong at low mileage.** At 15km a week, 10% is eight minutes at threshold — below the dose that produces the adaptation, against Daniels' own guidance that a tempo run is about twenty minutes. **v6 adds a time floor and ceiling per band: threshold lands between 20 and 30 minutes, interval between 12 and 15**, with the percentage binding only between them. Measured across every plan at minimum and maximum days: threshold 5.5–29.9 min (the 5.5 is a progressive long run's finishing segment in a taper week), interval 11.3–11.7 min, rep ≤8.7% on the smallest weeks (strides), long run 30.7–32.4% — the long run runs ~2 points over its 30% target because the two settling passes do not fully converge after quality is trimmed; left as is, since 32% of a three-day week is normal.

**4. Start Running was more aggressive than the plan it should have copied.** NHS Couch to 5K is **9 weeks**, and around **27% of people finish it as written**, injury and pace escalation being the usual reasons; the evidence-based alternatives are *slower* 10–12 week variants. PM-1240's version was 8 weeks and — worse — topped out at 2 × 10 min with a walk break in week 6 before asking for **25 minutes continuous** in week 7. NHS bridges that with 20 min continuous at week 5 run 3 and 25 min at week 6 run 3, using **three different sessions** in weeks 5 and 6. Ours repeated one session three times every week. **Re-authored on the NHS intervals verbatim** (migration `pm1241_start_running_nhs_shape`): 9 weeks, 13 run/walk templates, session totals now 29/29/25/31.5/31-31-30/34-33-35/35/38/40 minutes, matching NHS exactly.

**A fourth engine defect fell out of that.** The long-run row is pinned to the member's chosen day and the rest fill forward, so a member choosing Wednesday on a 3-day week got slot 3 — the breakthrough run — **first**. PM-1240 had also hung a fake `is_long_run` on run/walk weeks purely to stop the engine dropping a session. **v5 fixes the cause: when no row is flagged, every run day is available**, slot order is chronological, and the fake flag is gone from the catalogue. Verified with a Wednesday anchor: w5a/w5b/w5c land Wed/Fri/Sun in order.

**The strategic miss, which matters more than any of the above.** A meta-analysis of 26 RCTs puts **strength training at a ~50% reduction in overuse running injuries** — the largest effect anywhere in this literature, larger than pace prescription or graded progression. Novice runners take **17.8 injuries per 1000 hours against 7.7 for recreational runners**, and the 10% rule itself has weak support (the GRONORUN RCT found no difference between an 8-week standard and a 13-week 10%-graded programme). **So the product claim is not "our plans match Runna's". It is that we already ship the strength work, the habits and the mental-health side alongside the running, for £10 against their £15.99** — they bolted strength on; we would be adding running to a platform that already has the intervention that halves the injury rate.

Review sheet `docs/running-catalogue-review.html` regenerated and reframed for a CiRF reviewer rather than Calum.
